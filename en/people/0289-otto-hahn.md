# Otto Hahn

**Born:** 8 March 1879

**Died / status:** 28 July 1968

**Fields:** Radiochemistry, Nuclear physics, Nuclear fission

![Portrait of Otto Hahn](../portraits/30645633e4e1078106e0.jpg)

## Biography

Hahn was a German radiochemist who investigated radioactive nuclides in Berlin and later led the Max Planck Society. His long collaboration with Meitner preceded the chemical identification of barium with Strassmann in neutron-irradiated uranium, decisive evidence underlying the discovery of nuclear fission. He was awarded the 1944 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

## Major contributions to physics

- Discovered and studied radioactive nuclides with Meitner and other collaborators
- Identified barium in irradiated uranium with Strassmann, providing decisive experimental evidence of fission
- Developed radiochemical separation and tracer methods

## Historical and source notes

- The discovery of nuclear fission involved the chemical experiments of Hahn and Strassmann and the physical explanation by Meitner and Frisch; their distinct contributions should be retained.
