# Jean Buridan

**Born:** c. 1300

**Died / status:** c. 1358–1361

**Fields:** History of theories of motion, Natural philosophy

## Biography

Jean Buridan was a fourteenth-century French scholastic philosopher who taught for many years at the University of Paris and served twice as rector. Working within the Aristotelian tradition, he developed impetus as an explanation for a projectile's continued motion after release. His dates can only be estimated.

## Major contributions to physics

- Developed and disseminated impetus theory, explaining continued projectile motion through an acquired internal motive quality.
- Discussed relationships between impetus, quantity of matter, speed and resistance, leaving important questions for later theories of motion.

## Historical and source notes

- The Stanford Encyclopedia records him alive in 1358 and his benefice reassigned in 1361; death is therefore represented by an approximate interval.
