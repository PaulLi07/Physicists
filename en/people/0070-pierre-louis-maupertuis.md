**Born:** 28 September 1698

**Died / status:** 27 July 1759

## Biography

Maupertuis was a French mathematician and astronomer who led the Lapland meridian expedition and later headed the Berlin academy. He tested Newtonian predictions about Earth's figure and proposed an extremal principle for action. His philosophical writings and priority disputes influenced eighteenth-century science.

## Major contributions to physics

- Used Lapland measurements to support the flattening of Earth at the poles.
- Proposed an early principle of least action, advancing variational ideas in theories of nature.
