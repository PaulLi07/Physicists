# Hypatia

**Born:** c. 355–370 CE

**Died / status:** March 415 CE

**Fields:** Astronomy, History of mathematical physics

![Image associated with Hypatia](../portraits/998b9928c094c73bb5b4-feeb17668c.png)

An imagined representation of Hypatia by Jules Maurice Gaspard for a 1908 publication, about 1,500 years after her lifetime; it is not an ancient record of her appearance.

## Biography

Hypatia was a mathematician, teacher of astronomy and Neoplatonic philosopher in late antique Alexandria, and the daughter of Theon. She taught mathematics and philosophy and helped transmit astronomical learning. Her works and life must largely be reconstructed from later testimony. She was murdered by a mob in 415.

## Major contributions to physics

- Taught and transmitted mathematics used in astronomical calculation.
- Letters associated with her scholarly circle document knowledge of instruments such as astrolabes and hydrometers, without establishing that she invented them.

## Historical and source notes

- Birth is commonly estimated around 355 or 370; she died in March 415, without a securely established exact day.
- References in students’ letters do not establish that Hypatia invented the astrolabe or hydrometer.
