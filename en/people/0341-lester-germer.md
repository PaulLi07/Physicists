# Lester Germer

**Born:** 10 October 1896

**Died / status:** October 1971

**Fields:** Quantum physics, Surface physics

## Biography

An American experimental physicist, Germer spent much of his career at Bell Laboratories and continued research at Cornell after retirement. His electron-diffraction work with Clinton Davisson provided decisive experimental support for de Broglie's matter-wave hypothesis.

## Major contributions to physics

- Discovered electron diffraction from a nickel crystal with Davisson in 1927, confirming the wave nature of electrons.
- Developed electron-diffraction methods for studying crystals and surface structure.
- Investigated thermionic emission, metal surfaces and electrical-contact phenomena.

## Historical and source notes

- His full name was Lester Halbert Germer; Louis Germer is a mistaken identification.
- The death date is commonly given as 3 October 1971, whereas the Linda Hall Library biography gives 4 October. The canonical date retains month precision and records the discrepancy pending confirmation from a contemporary original record.
