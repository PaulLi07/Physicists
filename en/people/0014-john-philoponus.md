# John Philoponus

**Born:** c. 490 CE

**Died / status:** c. 570 CE

**Fields:** History of theories of motion, Natural philosophy

No verified portrait was obtained. This is the title page of the 1540 printed edition of Philoponus’s commentary on On Generation and Corruption (BEIC collection), a work image rather than a portrait.

## Biography

John Philoponus was a late antique Alexandrian philosopher and commentator on Aristotle, educated under Ammonius. He criticized inherited accounts of motion and the cosmos through argument and experience. His writings reached later natural philosophy through Syriac, Arabic and Latin traditions.

## Major contributions to physics

- Criticized accounts in which a projectile is continually pushed by the surrounding medium and discussed an imparted capacity for motion.
- Challenged a strict proportionality between falling speed and weight and considered the possibility of motion in a vacuum.

## Historical and source notes

- Life dates are approximate; his imparted motive capacity must not be equated with modern momentum or Newtonian inertia.
